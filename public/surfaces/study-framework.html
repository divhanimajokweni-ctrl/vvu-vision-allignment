Prepared for

Nelson Mandela University (NMU) — Office of the Deputy Vice-Chancellor: Research, Innovation and Development; Faculty of Engineering, the Built Environment and Information Technology; and the Centre for Water, Sanitation and Environmental Research (cWASER).

Prepared by

Venture Vision Ubuntu (VVU) — Engineering and Programmes Division. Authored by the VVU Principal Investigator in collaboration with the VVU Governance Counsel.

Purpose of this document

This Framework sets out, in plain institutional language, the scope, methods, deliverables, governance and timeline of a proposed multi-year study that would deploy the VVU verifiable-evidence stack across a defined set of water and sanitation infrastructure assets in the Nelson Mandela Bay Metropolitan Municipality and adjoining service areas. It is submitted to NMU, the Department of Water and Sanitation (DWS), the Nelson Mandela Bay Municipality, and to identified research partners as a basis for formal partnership negotiation.

The document is intended to be read in full before any memorandum of understanding is drafted. It is deliberately comprehensive: the five environments, the five deployments, the pilot programme, the research programme, the asks, the returns, the timeline, the anchor role, the governing structure and the study's stated aim are presented in a single coherent argument rather than split across annexures. This is so that every reviewer, regardless of which section they are responsible for, can read every other section their decision touches.

The Framework is not a contract. It is not a tender specification. It is not a procurement document. It is a study plan that, on approval, will give rise to a contract, a procurement plan and a research protocol — each separately negotiated and separately governed. Nothing in this document commits any party to anything. Everything in this document is, however, written as though it were already binding, so that reviewers can see exactly what they would be committing to.

Governing rules

The Framework is written under five governing rules, and every section of the document is answerable to them. They are stated here in the order in which they govern the work, not in the order in which they appear in the body.

  1. Verifiability. Every claim made by the system, about the system, is independently recomputable. No claim is published that cannot be re-derived from primary observations by a party other than the one that made the claim. This rule is the reason SHA-256, content-addressed storage, canonical JSON, Ed25519 signatures and a write-once read-many (WORM) ledger are present in the stack. They are not architectural ornaments; they are the load-bearing structure under the entire study.
  2. Provenance. Every observation, every artefact, every published statement carries a provenance class. There are four: device-observed, operator-authored, harness-generated and synthetic. No class is privileged over another; each is permitted in defined surfaces and forbidden in others. Provenance is the contract the system makes with its readers: it tells them, in machine-checkable form, where a thing came from.
  3. Voice law. The system refuses a small number of words and phrases — unassailable, fully operational, production-ready, certified — on the grounds that they over-claim. Refusing them is a constraint on the writers, not on the readers. The readers are entitled to language that does not promise more than the evidence can carry.
  4. Irreversibility with correction. Published material is never silently altered. Errors are corrected by versioned corrections that preserve the original; retractions are flagged, never removed. This rule applies to observations, to publications and to the ledger itself.
  5. Authorisation before action. No test, no adversarial case, no publication, no retraction runs without an explicit, recorded authorisation whose scope and time window are machine-checked at the moment of action. Authorisation is a precondition, not a record.

These five rules are referenced by name throughout the document. Where a section appears to conflict with one of them, the rule prevails and the section is to be read down to conform to it.

PART 1 What the Study Is

1.1 The study in one paragraph

The study proposes to install, instrument and operate the VVU verifiable-evidence stack across a defined set of water and sanitation infrastructure assets over a period of thirty months, and to do so in a manner that produces a reusable, independently verifiable evidence base concerning the condition, performance and failure modes of those assets.

The stack comprises five software environments (FORGE, BENCH, FIRE, FIELD, PRESS), five physical deployments (reservoir telemetry, treatment-plant instrumentation, distribution-network pressure logging, consumer-side flow monitoring, and an integrated municipal dashboard), a pilot programme of constrained scope, and a research programme whose findings will be published in the open literature and in machine-checkable form. The study is anchored at NMU, which provides physical facility space, faculty time and a long-duration institutional commitment; it is sponsored by DWS and the municipality, which provide the assets under study and the access to them; and it is partnered by research organisations whose role is to design the experimental protocol and to publish its findings.

1.2 What the study is not

The study is not a procurement of a water-management system. The municipality will continue to operate its existing SCADA, telemetry and billing systems; the study runs alongside these systems, drawing on their data where licensed to do so and instrumenting assets that the existing systems do not cover. The study is not a regulatory submission. DWS will receive the study's findings and may use them to inform future regulatory guidance, but the study itself does not produce regulatory text. The study is not a commercial pilot for the purposes of producing a marketable product. Although the VVU stack is a working system and will be operated as a working system throughout the study, the contract under which it is operated prohibits its sale, sublicensing or commercial deployment for the duration of the study and for a defined hold-out period thereafter. The study is not a security assessment of the municipality's existing systems. Although the VVU FIRE environment runs adversarial tests, those tests are confined to the VVU stack itself; no penetration or stress testing is performed against the municipality's operational systems.

1.3 Why a study, and not a deployment

A deployment, in the commercial sense, optimises for uptime, throughput and user satisfaction. A study optimises for the integrity of the evidence produced about the deployment. These are not the same thing. A deployment that achieves uptime by suppressing error logs produces no evidence about its failure modes; a study that achieves evidence integrity by running slowly and carefully may produce less throughput than a deployment. The study is shaped by the second of these priorities. Every design choice — the five-environment separation of concerns, the WORM ledger, the canonical-JSON content hashing, the refusal of voice-law-banned words, the requirement for explicit authorisation before any adversarial action — is made so that the evidence produced by the study can be inspected, re-derived and trusted by parties who were not present when the evidence was generated. A pure deployment does not need this; a study does.

1.4 The five questions the study asks

The study is organised around five questions. They are stated here in their plain form; Part 11 states them in their formal, testable form.

  1. Can a verifiable-evidence stack be operated, by an institution, over a long duration, at infrastructure scale, without the integrity of the evidence degrading?
  2. Where the stack fails — and it will fail, in ways both known and unknown — are the failure modes detectable by the stack itself, recoverable by the institution, and explainable to a third party?
  3. Does the separation of observation, verification, adversarial testing and publication into distinct environments, each with its own maxim and its own self-test suite, produce evidence that is more trustworthy than a single integrated system would produce?
  4. Does the cost of operating the stack at infrastructure scale fall within a defensible range relative to the value of the evidence produced, and does that cost decrease with the duration of operation?
  5. Can the stack, operated as a study, produce findings that are usable by a regulator (DWS), a service authority (the municipality), an academic community (NMU and partners) and the public, without the four audiences being able to extract different evidence from the same record?

1.5 The discipline the study imposes

The study imposes a discipline on itself that is heavier than the discipline a commercial deployment would impose. It does this for two reasons. First, the study's evidence is intended to be used by parties who did not participate in producing it — and the only way to make evidence usable at a distance is to make it difficult to tamper with locally. Second, the study is intended to fail visibly. The VVU FIRE environment exists to break the VVU BENCH environment in defined ways, so that the conditions under which BENCH holds are made explicit. A study that hid its own failure modes would defeat its purpose. The discipline — voice law, authorisation gates, version preservation, provenance labelling, canonical hashing, append-only logging — is the means by which the study makes its failure modes visible.

PART 2 The Five Environments

The VVU stack is structured as five environments, each with a single responsibility, a single maxim, a self-test suite and a gap register. The environments are not layers in a stack; they are stations on a circuit. An artefact moves through them, and at each station it acquires a different kind of integrity. The separation is deliberate: it prevents the integrity of one station from being compromised by the workload of another.

FORGE spec · VVU-ENV-FORGE-2026-001 · accent purple
"status may only be PROPOSED"

FORGE is the environment in which artefacts are authored. An artefact, in VVU terms, is anything the system produces: a sensor reading envelope, a learning module, a spreadsheet row, a plugin scaffold, a harness run. FORGE writes these artefacts, hashes them and signs them, but it does not assert their truth. The maxim — status may only be PROPOSED — is the contract FORGE makes with the rest of the stack: anything that emerges from FORGE is a proposal, not a finding. The truth of an artefact is established downstream, in BENCH, or not at all.

FORGE carries four provenance classes (device-observed, operator-authored, harness-generated, synthetic) and refuses to write any artefact whose provenance is not declared. It carries four surfaces (Plugin-Creator-Studio, STUDI, spreadsheet, Validation-Dashboard), each of which is a defined mode of authoring with its own permitted operations. FORGE's self-test suite runs twelve checks on every mount: SHA-256 known-answer tests, round-trip recomputation, PROPOSED-only enforcement against six forbidden status values, canonical key-sort invariance, single-byte digest divergence, envelope self-verify, provenance-class coverage, surface coverage, voice law, and a store round-trip. The verdict is binary: ALL 12 CHECKS PASSED, or a count of failures.

The gap register in FORGE tracks six items: artefact creation, proposed-claim enforcement, plugin studio, STUDI module review, spreadsheet rows, and validation-dash runs. Each item is marked RECONSTRUCTED (carried over from a reference design and rebuilt), BUILT (newly implemented to spec), ABSENT (planned but not implemented), UNREACHABLE (planned but blocked), or SPEC-ONLY (defined but not built). The register is the FORGE team's standing audit of its own completeness; it is published with the study.

BENCH spec · VVU-ENV-BENCH-2026-001 · accent green
"verification is recomputation"

BENCH is the environment in which artefacts from FORGE are verified. Verification, in VVU terms, is not a label; it is the act of recomputing a claim from its primary inputs and comparing the result with the claim as published. BENCH holds the WORM ledger — the write-once read-many chain that records every verified artefact — and the Ed25519 signature keys that bind each verified entry to its verifier. BENCH holds two verification policies: the chain-linkage policy (which checks that each row's prev_hash matches the previous row's digest) and the payload-hash policy (which checks that each row's payload, when canonicalised and hashed, matches the row's stored digest). Both policies are applied on every verification, and both must pass for an artefact to be marked VERIFIED.

BENCH's self-test suite runs nineteen checks, including the SHA-256 known-answer test against the empty input and the string "abc", the Ed25519 sign-then-verify round trip, the chain-linkage and payload-hash policy checks, the tamper-detection test (in which a single byte is flipped in a row and the chain correctly refuses to verify), the canonical-JSON key-sort invariance test, the voice-law test, and a store round-trip. The verdict is binary: ALL 19 CHECKS PASSED, or a count of failures. The gap register in BENCH tracks six items: chain integrity, policy enforcement, verification workflow, the independent verifier, self-test coverage and the operation log.

FIRE spec · VVU-ENV-FIRE-2026-001 · accent red
"Fire never emits PROVEN"

FIRE is the environment in which the VVU stack is deliberately broken, in defined ways, so that the conditions under which BENCH holds are made explicit. FIRE runs two kinds of cases: TAMPER cases, in which the WORM chain is mutated in a defined way and BENCH is asked to verify the mutated chain; and GOVERNANCE cases, in which an artefact envelope is malformed in a defined way and the governance engine is asked to accept or reject it. There are nine TAMPER cases and nine GOVERNANCE cases, eighteen in total, each with a defined expectation and a defined counterexample if the expectation is met.

FIRE's maxim — Fire never emits PROVEN — is the contract FIRE makes with the rest of the stack. FIRE runs tests, generates counterexamples, produces reports, but it never asserts that anything has been proven. The strongest claim FIRE makes is PASS-UNDER-CONDITIONS, and that claim is bounded by the conditions of the test. FIRE's self-test suite runs fifteen checks, including a deliberate-failure test (in which a vulnerable build of the chain correctly fails to detect a tamper case and a counterexample is preserved), an honest-build test (in which the same case correctly passes), a control test, an authorisation-gate test, a coverage-statement test, and the SHA-256 known-answer test. The gap register in FIRE tracks eight items, including the six honesty-boundary items and two service-level checks (SVC-3004 and SVC-3005).

FIELD spec · VVU-ENV-FIELD-2026-001 · accent yellow
"observation precedes interpretation"

FIELD is the environment in which observations are taken. An observation, in VVU terms, is a reading from a physical sensor, captured at a defined time, in a defined unit, with a defined provenance. FIELD holds the sensor registry, the observation log, the field-operator authorisations, and the intake queue for observations awaiting verification. FIELD does not interpret the observations it holds; interpretation is a downstream activity that happens in PRESS, after the observations have been verified in BENCH. The maxim — observation precedes interpretation — is the contract FIELD makes with the rest of the stack: nothing reaches the interpretive layer that has not been recorded as an observation with a provenance class.

FIELD's gap register tracks six items: the sensor registry, the observation log, the operator authorisation workflow, the verification intake queue, the field-to-BENCH handoff, and the self-test coverage. FIELD's self-test suite runs fourteen checks, including sensor-registry integrity, observation-shape validation, time-window enforcement, provenance-class enforcement, the canonical-JSON known-answer test, and the SHA-256 known-answer test.

PRESS spec · VVU-ENV-PRESS-2026-001 · accent blue
"publication is irreversible"

PRESS is the environment in which verified material is drafted, approved, published and, where necessary, corrected or retracted. PRESS holds drafts, receipts (which are references to BENCH verification outcomes), publications, corrections, retractions, and consent records. PRESS extracts statements from draft text, classifies each statement as OBSERVATION, INTERPRETATION or CONCLUSION by a defined rule set, and applies a verification-language gate: a statement may use the word "verified" only if a PROVEN receipt is bound to the publication, and only within the scope that the receipt permits. The maxim — publication is irreversible — is the contract PRESS makes with the rest of the stack: what is published cannot be silently altered; it can only be versioned, corrected or retracted.

PRESS's self-test suite runs sixteen checks, including the INCONCLUSIVE-receipt-forbids-verification-language test, the missing-receipt test, the PROVEN-receipt-permits-exactly-one-bounded-sentence test, the expired-receipt test, the dangling-reference test, the operator-override test, the correction-preserves-original test, the retraction-flags-but-never-removes test, the consent-gate test, the version-hash-recomputation test, the voice-law test, the SHA-256 known-answer test, and the store round-trip. The gap register in PRESS tracks six items, including the reader-facing media, the VVU command centre, the outreach panel, the compose pipeline, the magazine masters, and the editorial workflow.

2.6 The circuit

An artefact begins in FORGE, where it is authored with a declared provenance and a status of PROPOSED. It moves to FIELD, where — if it is a sensor envelope — it is recorded as an observation. It moves to BENCH, where it is verified by recomputation. It moves to FIRE, where the conditions under which BENCH holds are tested by deliberate tampering. It moves to PRESS, where — if it has been verified — it may be drafted into a publication, approved under a bound receipt, and published. At each station, the artefact acquires a different kind of integrity: in FORGE, the integrity of authorship; in FIELD, the integrity of observation; in BENCH, the integrity of verification; in FIRE, the integrity of adversarial coverage; in PRESS, the integrity of publication. No single environment is sufficient. The integrity of the artefact is the product of the circuit.

Architectural Note
The five-environment separation is not a layering. FORGE does not call BENCH; BENCH does not call FIRE. Each environment operates against a shared, content-addressed store and emits its own append-only log. The integrity of the circuit is the integrity of the artefact's hash as it moves between environments — a hash that no environment can alter, and that every environment recomputes on intake.

PART 4 The Pilot Programme

4.1 The pilot's purpose

The pilot programme is the constrained first phase of the study. Its purpose is not to deliver value to the buyer; it is to establish, before any infrastructure is instrumented, that the VVU stack can be operated by the study team, at the study site, against a single defined asset, in a manner that produces verifiable evidence. The pilot is, in effect, the study's proof of its own method. If the pilot fails, the study is suspended; if the pilot succeeds in defined terms, the study proceeds to the first deployment.

4.2 The pilot's scope

The pilot instruments a single asset — the North Basin reservoir, one of the four reservoirs in deployment D-1 — with a defined sensor set: one ultrasonic level sensor, one pressure sensor, one temperature sensor, one turbidity sensor and one chlorine-residual sensor. The pilot runs for ninety days. During the pilot, the sensor set is operated continuously; observations are taken at five-minute intervals; the WORM ledger is maintained continuously; and the PRESS environment publishes one weekly summary, on a defined day and at a defined time, drawn from the verified observations of the preceding week.

The pilot is operated by a defined team: a VVU field engineer (operator-authored provenance), an NMU-attached graduate intern (operator-authored provenance, training role), a municipal site engineer (device-observed provenance, sensor maintenance), and a DWS observer (read-only, no authorisation to act). The team's composition is fixed for the duration of the pilot; substitution is permitted only with the written agreement of all four parties.

4.3 The pilot's success criteria

The pilot is deemed to have succeeded when all six of the following criteria are met, and not before:

  1. Continuous operation. The sensor set operates for at least 95% of the ninety-day pilot window, with any downtime recorded, explained and recovered from.
  2. Evidence completeness. At least 95% of the expected observations are taken, recorded in FIELD, verified in BENCH, and present in the WORM ledger.
  3. Verification integrity. The WORM chain passes the chain-linkage and payload-hash policy checks on every recomputation performed during the pilot, with no row added, removed or altered.
  4. FIRE coverage. At least one TAMPER case and one GOVERNANCE case are run against the pilot's chain each week, with the expected outcome in each case and a counterexample preserved in the event of any unexpected outcome.
  5. PRESS publication. Each weekly summary is published, with a bound PROVEN receipt where appropriate, with no use of voice-law-banned words, and with all CONCLUSION statements supported by a bound PROVEN receipt.
  6. Self-test passage. Every self-test suite, in every environment, runs on every mount during the pilot, with the verdict ALL N CHECKS PASSED recorded in the operation log for each run.

If any criterion fails, the pilot is deemed to have failed, and the study is suspended pending review. The review is conducted by NMU, DWS and the municipality jointly, and its outcome is one of three: re-run the pilot with defined corrections, re-scope the study, or terminate the study. The VVU team does not have a casting vote in the review.

PART 5 The Research Programme

5.1 The research programme's purpose

The research programme runs alongside the deployments from Month 6 of the study to Month 30. Its purpose is to take the evidence produced by the deployments and the pilot and to publish findings, in the open literature and in machine-checkable form, that contribute to the body of knowledge concerning the long-duration operation of verifiable-evidence systems at infrastructure scale. The research programme is the reason NMU is the anchor institution: the programme requires faculty time, graduate supervision, institutional ethics clearance, and a long-duration commitment that only a university can provide.

5.2 The research programme's structure

The research programme is structured as five work-streams, each with a defined lead, a defined deliverable and a defined publication target. The work-streams are independent of the deployments — they draw on the deployments' evidence but do not gate the deployments' operation. The leads are nominated by NMU and approved by the study's governing structure (Part 10).

Work-stream Lead Deliverable Publication target
W-1 NMU — Computer Science A longitudinal analysis of the WORM ledger's integrity across the thirty-month study, with a quantified account of every failure mode observed. 2 peer-reviewed papers, 1 conference paper, machine-checkable ledger release.
W-2 NMU — cWASER, with DWS participation A taxonomy of failure modes observed in water and sanitation infrastructure, classified by provenance class and by detection path. 1 peer-reviewed paper, 1 technical report to DWS.
W-3 NMU — Engineering Economics, with municipal participation A cost analysis of the VVU stack at infrastructure scale, with a comparison against the cost of the existing SCADA and telemetry estate. 1 peer-reviewed paper, 1 technical report to the municipality.
W-4 NMU — Computer Science, with VVU participation A comparative analysis of the five-environment separation against a single integrated system, drawing on the FIRE test results. 1 peer-reviewed paper, 1 conference paper.
W-5 NMU — cWASER, with municipal participation A study of the extent to which the public can read, understand and trust the evidence produced by the stack, with a defined survey protocol. 1 peer-reviewed paper, 1 public-facing report.

Each work-stream is reviewed annually by the governing structure, and each work-stream's deliverables are released under a Creative Commons Attribution licence, with the machine-checkable artefacts released under an open-source licence. No work-stream is permitted to publish findings drawn from the study's evidence that conflict with the evidence as recorded in the WORM ledger; where a work-stream's analysis differs from the ledger, the analysis must state the difference explicitly.

5.3 Ethics and clearance

The research programme is subject to NMU's research ethics committee, and the pilot programme is subject to the same committee's expedited review. The ethics clearance covers the collection of sensor data from municipal assets, the publication of aggregated findings, and the conduct of the public-readability survey (W-5). The clearance does not cover the collection of personally identifiable information at consumer properties; deployment D-4 operates with the explicit, opt-in, recorded consent of the property owners, and the consent records are held in the PRESS environment under the same consent-gate rules that govern publication.

Open-Licencing Principle
All work-stream deliverables are released under a Creative Commons Attribution licence; all machine-checkable artefacts are released under an open-source licence. No work-stream may publish findings drawn from the study's evidence that conflict with the evidence as recorded in the WORM ledger. Where a work-stream's analysis differs from the ledger, the analysis must state the difference explicitly.
PART 6 What NMU Is Being Asked For

6.1 The anchor role

NMU is asked to serve as the anchor institution for the study. The anchor role is defined in detail in Part 9; in summary, it comprises the provision of physical facility space (a defined room for the VVU stack's compute and storage), faculty time (the work-stream leads and graduate supervision), institutional commitment (a thirty-month binding commitment that survives changes of personnel), and the institutional credibility that the study needs in order to be reviewed by DWS and the municipality.

6.2 The specific asks

The specific asks of NMU are six:

  1. Facility. A defined room, of approximately 40 square metres, on NMU's North Campus or South Campus, with the power, cooling and network connectivity specified in Appendix B, for the duration of the study and for a defined hold-out period thereafter.
  2. Faculty time. The commitment of one full-time equivalent senior academic, across the five work-stream leads, for the duration of the study. The senior academic is nominated by NMU; the VVU team does not nominate.
  3. Graduate supervision. The registration of three master's candidates and one doctoral candidate, on topics drawn from the five work-streams, with VVU personnel serving as co-supervisors where the candidates' committees so elect.
  4. Ethics clearance. The processing of the research programme's ethics clearance through NMU's research ethics committee, on the timeline specified in Appendix C, with VVU providing all supporting documentation.
  5. Institutional commitment. A thirty-month binding commitment, in the form of a memorandum of understanding, that survives changes of personnel at NMU, at VVU, at DWS and at the municipality.

PART 10 The Governing Structure

10.1 The governing structure

The study is governed by a four-party structure: NMU, VVU, DWS and the municipality. Each party nominates two representatives to a Study Steering Committee, which meets monthly for the first six months of the study, quarterly thereafter, and at any other time at the request of any party. The Steering Committee's decisions are taken by simple majority, with the chair rotating annually between the parties in the order NMU, DWS, municipality, VVU. The chair does not have a casting vote; in the event of a tie, the question is referred back to the parties for resolution.

10.2 The Steering Committee's authority

The Steering Committee's authority is defined and limited. It comprises:

  2. Approval of deployment commencement. The Steering Committee approves, by vote, the commencement of each deployment (D-1 through D-5), on the basis of the success criteria of the preceding deployment or the pilot.
  3. Approval of the research programme. The Steering Committee approves, by vote, the appointment of the work-stream leads, the annual review of the work-streams, and the release of the work-stream deliverables.

APPENDIX E Voice Law

E.1 The banned words

The VVU stack refuses, by mechanical enforcement, the following words and phrases in any output it produces: unassailable, fully operational, production-ready, certified. The refusal is implemented in the voiceLawViolation function in the VVU shared library (src/lib/vvu-utils.ts), which is called by every environment before any output is emitted. The refusal is a constraint on the writers of the system, not on the readers; the readers are entitled to language that does not over-claim.

E.2 The reasons

Each banned word is banned for a defined reason:

  • unassailable — over-claims the strength of the evidence; the evidence is recomputable, which is a weaker and more useful property than unassailability.
  • fully operational — over-claims the completeness of the system; the system is operated under defined conditions, not fully.
  • production-ready — over-claims the maturity of the system; the system is study-ready, which is a different and more honest property.
  • certified — over-claims the authority of the verifier; the verifier recomputes, which is a different and more verifiable act than certifying.

E.3 The enforcement

The enforcement is mechanical: the function checks, case-insensitively, for the presence of the banned words in any string passed to it, and returns a violation record if any are present. The function is called by every environment's publication path, by every environment's self-test suite, and by the BENCH verification path. A violation in any of these paths blocks the operation that would have produced the banned word, and the violation is recorded in the operation log. The enforcement is not a guideline; it is a precondition.

E.4 The exceptions

There are no exceptions. The banned words are banned in all outputs, in all environments, at all times. Where a banned word appears in an input — for example, in a sensor reading envelope whose operator-authored provenance includes a comment field — the input is accepted, but the comment field is stripped from any downstream output. Where a banned word appears in a quoted text — for example, in a PRESS draft that quotes a regulatory text — the quoted text is permitted, but the quotation is marked as a quotation in the PRESS environment's extraction rules, and the quotation is not classified as a CONCLUSION by the rule engine.

Voice Law Principle
The banned words are banned in all outputs, in all environments, at all times. There are no exceptions. Where a banned word appears in an input, the input is accepted but the offending field is stripped from downstream output. Where a banned word appears in a quotation, the quotation is permitted but is marked as such and is not classified as a CONCLUSION by the rule engine. The constraint sits on the writers, not on the readers — readers are entitled to language that does not over-claim.
